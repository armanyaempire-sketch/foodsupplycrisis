## Editorial rules for future articles

1. Before drafting, choose one primary keyword and write down the exact question the page answers. Check this map first; do not create a second page for an intent already owned by an existing article.
2. Keep the primary phrase natural in the SEO title, H1 or opening paragraph. Use secondary phrases only where the article genuinely answers them.
3. Use a unique, accurate meta description (80–180 characters in the current content schema). Google may still generate a query-specific snippet from page content.
4. Give every article a different evidence base, examples, calculations, comparison or decision aid. Rewording the same generic section is not differentiation.
5. Link to complementary pages with descriptive anchor text. Do not force every article to link to every other article.
6. Set `updatedDate` only after a material evidence or analysis change; do not change dates for cosmetic edits.
7. Do not add a `meta keywords` tag. The structured `primaryKeyword` and `keywords` fields are used for editorial planning and Article JSON-LD, not as a claim that Google ranks pages by a meta-keywords field.

## Measurement limitation

This keyword map is based on distinct article topics and search intent. Search-volume, competition and current query-to-page performance have not been validated with this site's Search Console data in this pass because the connected GSC Wizard account currently requires an active subscription. Do not treat these targets as measured search-volume opportunities. After Search Console access is restored, review query impressions and page overlap and adjust the map to actual search demand.
