> **Update — 13 November 2026:** This rewrite adds Conab's first 2026–27 crop estimate and the IBGE October price release. Conab's figures are early-season forecasts, while IPCA measures consumer prices; they describe different parts of the food system.

## Key takeaways

- Conab's first estimate for the 2026–27 grain crop is **367.1 million tonnes**, up 1.4% from the previous cycle, with planted area expected to reach 85.4 million hectares.
- The national output forecast is strong, but the crop mix is uneven: Conab expects growth in soybeans and maize while rice and especially wheat are forecast to decline.
- Consumer food prices rose faster than the all-items CPI in October. The IBGE food and beverages group increased 1.04% in the month, and food at home increased 1.31%.

## Crop outlook: 367.1 million tonnes is a forecast, not a harvest result

Brazil's National Supply Company (Conab) published its first estimate for the 2026–27 grain crop on 15 October. It put production at 367.1 million tonnes, 1.4% above the 2025–26 cycle, and expected planted area to expand 2.3% to 85.4 million hectares.

The estimate matters because Brazil supplies large volumes of globally traded crops. A change in its soybean or maize outlook can affect feed, oilseed and food markets beyond the country's borders. But this is the first estimate of the new crop cycle. Planting, weather, input use, yield outcomes and harvest progress will all affect the final number.

| Crop / indicator | Conab first estimate for 2026–27 | Change from previous cycle |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total grains | 367.1 Mt | +1.4% |
| Planted area | 85.4 million ha | +2.3% |
| Soybeans | 181.6 Mt | +0.7% |
| Maize | 148.0 Mt | +2.4% |
| Rice | 10.8 Mt | −2.9% |
| Wheat | 5.7 Mt | −27.2% |

A national total can rise while individual crops contract. This distinction matters for Brazil's internal food basket and for export buyers. Soybeans and maize dominate the headline volume, but the rice and wheat outlook has a different implication for staple availability, milling and import/export balances.

## The crop mix explains why a record-scale total does not answer every food question

Soybeans and maize together make up most of the volume in Conab's first estimate. Their production is important to animal feed, vegetable oil, export earnings and domestic agribusiness. A modest change in these large crops can move the aggregate by millions of tonnes.

Rice, by contrast, is a smaller share of total grain tonnage but a staple in domestic consumption. Conab's 10.8 Mt estimate is 2.9% below the previous cycle. That does not automatically establish a shortage: stocks, imports, milling, regional harvest timing and household demand also matter. It does mean that a headline about record total grains should not be used as a substitute for examining the rice balance.

The wheat forecast is more sharply lower at 5.7 Mt, down 27.2%. Brazil's wheat market is structurally different from its soybean and maize markets: domestic production is concentrated in particular regions and the country has relied on imports to supply part of consumption. A weaker wheat crop can raise the importance of import availability, exchange rates, freight and quality requirements. It is not a direct prediction that bread prices will rise by the same percentage.

## Food CPI: a different signal from the farm forecast

IBGE's October IPCA, released on 12 November, showed overall monthly inflation of 0.14%, down from 0.82% in September. The food and beverages group nevertheless recorded the largest group increase at 1.04%, contributing 0.22 percentage points to the overall index. Food at home rose 1.31% in the month.

Several fresh foods recorded particularly large monthly moves: tomatoes increased 19.57%, onions 11.29%, rice 4.81% and meat 2.27%. Carrots, English potatoes and ground coffee declined. That spread is a practical demonstration of why there is no single “Brazil food price”: the change depends on the item and the consumer's basket.

The divergence between strong aggregate grain output and rising grocery prices is not a contradiction. The Conab production forecast mainly covers major grains and refers to a future harvest. The IPCA records prices paid by consumers across a wider basket and a current month. Fresh produce prices can move because of weather, local supply, seasonality and transport even while soybean and maize production prospects are favourable.

The next analytical question is persistence. If the October increases in tomato, onion or rice reverse as seasonal supply changes, the household effect may be temporary. If increases broaden across staples and persist over several releases, affordability becomes a more serious concern.

## The basic basket shows how location shapes the consumer experience

Conab and DIEESE's October 2026 basic food basket survey provides a useful city-level counterpart to national IPCA. In São Paulo, the basket was reported at R$951.37, up 3.13% month to month and 12.30% year on year. That is one city basket, not a national average and not a direct measure of every Brazilian household's spending.

The value of this measure is geographic detail. A national CPI can smooth out sharp movements in a city or commodity; a city basket can show how much a set of commonly purchased items costs locally. The two measures should be used together, not substituted for one another.

For a household, the price of rice, beans, meat, tomatoes and cooking oil matters more than the aggregate number of tonnes harvested. For producers and exporters, the crop estimate and input costs are more relevant. A good food-security analysis tracks both sides.

## Fertiliser dependence: a cost and timing issue

Brazil is a major agricultural producer but remains exposed to imported fertiliser. The Brazilian Government's National Fertilizer Plan, launched in 2022, described the country as importing about 85% of the fertilisers used in agriculture. That figure is a structural baseline from the plan, not a precise real-time import share for the 2026–27 season.

This exposure matters because fertiliser is purchased before and during the crop cycle. Higher landed prices can change farmer margins, the choice of crop, the rate of application and the timing of purchases. A disruption can also affect crop production with a lag: if an input arrives after the relevant planting or application window, a later shipment may not fully restore the lost yield potential.

However, it would be misleading to infer a current fertiliser shortage from import dependence alone. The relevant evidence is shipment volume, port and inland logistics, domestic stocks, retail availability and the timing of deliveries relative to the crop calendar. Brazil's crop outlook must be revised as that evidence develops.

## How Brazil compares: output and affordability are moving on different tracks

Brazil's current picture is a useful example of the difference between national food production and consumer affordability. The first Conab estimate shows total grain production growing 1.4%, while October's food and beverage prices rose 1.04% in one month. The two figures measure different things and can move in opposite directions without either being wrong.

This is also why Brazil should not be compared with a humanitarian food emergency solely by looking at crop tonnes. The country's export capacity, domestic distribution, consumer income and social protection are distinct parts of the food-security equation. The presence of a large harvest does not ensure that every household can afford a nutritious diet; higher retail prices do not prove that the country lacks food nationally.

A stronger assessment would track the final crop results alongside regional wholesale and retail prices, household income and the composition of the food basket. Those are the indicators that show whether pressure is temporary, concentrated in a few products or becoming broad-based.

## What to watch next

- Conab's subsequent monthly crop surveys: revisions to planted area, sowing progress and crop conditions, especially for rice, wheat, soybeans and maize.
- The next IBGE IPCA release: whether the October fresh-food increases persist, reverse or broaden into other categories.
- Conab/DIEESE city basket updates: which capitals show the largest cost changes, and whether food-basket pressure is regional or widespread.
- Fertiliser import and delivery information: prices and arrival timing before key application windows.

## If you live in Brazil

Use IBGE's item-level CPI and Conab/DIEESE city basket data to understand the local cost of food. The October numbers show why a national harvest headline cannot tell a household what happened to rice, tomatoes or meat prices in its own city. ## Assessment

Brazil's first 2026–27 grain forecast is large and slightly higher than the previous cycle, but the crop mix is not uniform. At the same time, October consumer food prices increased faster than the overall CPI. The current evidence supports a story about crop-specific output, input dependence and affordability—not a claim that Brazil is approaching a nationwide food shortage.
