> **Update — 19 November 2026:** Replaced the expired June–August 2026 projection with Nigeria’s latest Cadre Harmonisé current and projected figures, and kept their assessment periods explicit.

Nigeria is one of the countries where acute food insecurity is deteriorating even though the national food system is far larger and more diverse than the crisis areas suggest.

The latest FAO-WFP Hunger Hotspots assessment places particular concern on the northeast, especially Borno State.

## What the latest national assessment says

Nigeria's Cadre Harmonisé analysis for **October–December 2026** estimates that **26.8 million people** across 26 states and the Federal Capital Territory are in Crisis (CH Phase 3) or worse. This current-period estimate includes 756,815 internally displaced people in Borno, Sokoto and Zamfara.

The same assessment projects **33.5 million people** in Crisis or worse for **June–August 2027**, including 891,228 internally displaced people in those three states. These are separate time periods: the second number is a forecast for the 2027 lean season, not the current October–December count.

The older 34.8 million estimate and 15,000 Catastrophe figure referred to June–August 2026 and should not be presented as the latest national picture. The Cadre Harmonisé uses its own phase system; preserve the assessment date and geographic coverage whenever quoting its totals.

## Why Borno is vulnerable

Conflict and displacement have weakened livelihoods and market access across parts of the northeast.

Families that once relied on farming, livestock or local commerce may lose access to those income sources.

Markets can also become more expensive or difficult to reach when insecurity disrupts roads and transport.

## Why funding is important

Humanitarian operations depend on timely funding.

When resources are constrained, agencies may have to reduce the scale or coverage of assistance.

That can matter even when national food production is relatively stable because the people most at risk often cannot purchase food without support.

## Climate can add pressure

Northern Nigeria is exposed to weather variability.

Poor rainfall can reduce crop and pasture conditions. Flooding can damage fields and infrastructure.

But climate is only one part of the crisis. Conflict, displacement, poverty and market access determine whether a production shock becomes severe hunger.

## What would improve the outlook?

Immediate priorities include reliable humanitarian access, food and nutrition assistance, clean water and health services, and support for livelihoods where security allows.

Longer-term recovery depends on restoring markets, agricultural livelihoods and infrastructure.

## What readers should monitor

Watch food-security classifications, displacement, local prices, humanitarian funding, crop conditions and changes in security.

For regional context, see our [Hunger Hotspots 2026](/articles/hunger-hotspots-2026-13-countries/) article.



## Bottom line

A resilient response is simple: verify the risk, inventory what you already have, secure safe water, maintain several days of familiar shelf-stable food, protect refrigeration, rotate supplies and follow official guidance. Do not turn uncertainty into panic buying. The goal is to make a real disruption manageable while avoiding waste, financial strain and unnecessary pressure on the local food system.

## Why the lean-season period and state-level geography matter

A national projection can describe the scale of need, but it does not show where the most severe conditions are concentrated or whether they are expected to worsen in the same months. Nigeria's seasonal pattern, conflict-affected areas, displacement, market access and household purchasing power vary by state. A single countrywide headline can hide those differences.

Keep each estimate attached to its assessment window. An observed or projected figure for October–December cannot be compared directly with a June–August lean-season projection as though both describe the same moment. Also retain the classification system: Cadre Harmonisé and IPC outputs should be identified by name, reference period and phase definitions. Where a source changes its geographic coverage or includes internally displaced people differently, say so before interpreting the difference as an improvement or deterioration.

Readers should look for the original state-level map, population table, drivers and access notes. In Borno and other conflict-affected areas, insecurity and restricted movement may prevent food from reaching households even when supplies exist elsewhere in the country. The appropriate interpretation is therefore local and time-bound, not a blanket claim that all of Nigeria is facing the same emergency.
