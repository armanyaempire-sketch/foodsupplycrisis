Famine is not a single global event. It is a severe food-security condition that can emerge when people lose access to food because of conflict, displacement, weather, economic collapse or restrictions on humanitarian assistance.

In 2026, Sudan, Yemen, Somalia and Gaza remain among the places requiring close attention.

## Sudan

FAO and WFP identify Sudan as one of the world's most critical hunger hotspots.

The agencies reported that 19.5 million people faced high acute food insecurity through May 2026, while multiple areas were at risk of Famine under defined assumptions.

Conflict is the central driver, but the effects spread through markets, displacement and humanitarian access.

## Yemen

Yemen continues to face severe food insecurity after years of conflict and economic disruption.

Millions are projected to face Crisis or worse conditions.

The challenge includes household purchasing power as well as physical availability of food.

## Somalia

Somalia's risk is strongly connected to repeated drought, low crop production, conflict and economic pressure.

FAO and WFP projected millions facing high acute food insecurity during 2026 and identified Famine risk in a local district.

The country's experience shows how repeated climate shocks can erode resilience over time.

## Gaza

The situation in Gaza remains fragile.

The June 2026 Hunger Hotspots report noted major food-security improvements after the October 2025 ceasefire but said the earlier analysis had identified a Famine risk through mid-April.

The report did not provide a new full-period projection for June-November at drafting.

This is why dates matter when reporting Gaza food-security figures.

## Why the countries should not be lumped together

The underlying drivers differ.

Sudan is dominated by conflict and access.

Yemen combines conflict, economic weakness and humanitarian pressure.

Somalia has a major drought and livelihood component alongside conflict.

Gaza's conditions are shaped by conflict, access and the availability of essential supplies.

The appropriate response therefore differs across locations.

## What “Famine risk” means

A risk projection is not the same as a confirmed Famine classification.

IPC analysis uses defined thresholds and evidence.

Good reporting keeps the distinction visible because the consequences and urgency depend on what has actually been measured.

## What readers can do with the data

The most useful action is to follow current assessments from IPC, WFP and FAO rather than relying on old viral posts.

Country-level updates should be read by date and geographic coverage.

## Bottom line

The severe hunger emergencies of 2026 are real, but they are not one uniform phenomenon.

Understanding where famine risk is highest requires looking at the latest assessment, the affected population and the specific reasons access to food has failed.
